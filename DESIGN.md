# Design System: Marta Axpe — The Working Argument

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Working Argument"**

Every surface in this system makes one claim and then shows its proof. A slide, a portfolio page or a website section leads with a statement in confident display type, and everything underneath exists to support it: evidence in plain body text, the source of that evidence marked with a tag, and the risk named out loud in its own box. The system came out of a case study about "the end of average", and it holds itself to the same standard: specific over generic, deliberate over default.

The world is navy ink on cool white paper, with a single warm coral voice. Navy carries authority and almost all of the text; coral marks the one thing on a surface that deserves attention: an accent word in a headline, a section kicker, the outcome node in a diagram. Structure comes from hairlines, generous margins and flat circles with soft halo rings, never from shadows or decorative containers. Monospace appears only where something is labelled, numbered or classified, like notes in the margin of a working document.

The density is presentation-first: big type, wide margins, one idea per surface. On the web and in a portfolio the same rules hold at a smaller scale; the hierarchy and the voice don't change.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One claim per surface, carried by a Bricolage Grotesque headline that reads as a sentence.
- Navy ink for text and structure; coral used sparingly as the single accent voice.
- Evidence is labelled: Experience example, Hypothesis and Risk tags say where each claim comes from.
- Flat surfaces, hairline dividers and circle diagrams with halo rings; no drop shadows.
- Monospace only for labels, kickers, numbers and tags.

## Colors

A cool, inky palette with one warm voice: navy does the work, coral makes the point.

### Primary
- **Ink Navy** (`ink-navy`): the system's voice. All primary text, headlines, filled diagram nodes, dark cover and closing surfaces, and the "first move" panel. It is the default colour for anything that needs authority.

### Secondary
- **Signal Coral** (`coral`): the accent, used as a *fill* or on navy: accent nodes, the coral half of the time bars, kicker rules, accent words on dark surfaces. Never used as text on a light background; it fails contrast there.
- **Display Coral** (`coral-display`): coral for large accent words (40px and above) on light surfaces, such as "specific", "follow" or "mandate" inside a headline. Also the section numeral, at 16% opacity.
- **Text Coral** (`coral-text`): coral for small text on light surfaces: kickers, "What it's not" lines, arrows, column headers, the risk label.
- **Deep Coral** (`coral-deep`): coral for small text on the darker `cloud` panels, where Text Coral drops below 4.5:1.

### Neutral
- **Slate** (`slate`): secondary text: footers, sub-labels, captions, metadata.
- **Blush** (`blush`): the warm tint behind risk boxes and risk tags, and the halo ring around coral nodes.
- **Periwinkle** (`periwinkle`): the cool halo ring around navy nodes and the fill for secondary behaviour pills.
- **Hairline** (`hairline`): 1px dividers between rows, sections and table lines.
- **Paper** (`paper`): the default surface for content slides and pages; also text on navy.
- **Mist** (`mist`): the quiet surface for opener slides and cards that need to separate from the page.
- **Cloud** (`cloud`): a slightly deeper panel for grouped content, such as the local column of a framework.

### Named Rules
**The One Voice Rule.** Coral is the only warm colour and it marks one thing per area of a surface. If two coral elements compete for attention, one of them should be navy.

**The Coral-by-Background Rule.** Pick the coral by what it sits on, not by taste: `coral` on navy or as a fill, `coral-display` for words of 40px and over on light surfaces, `coral-text` for smaller text on paper or mist, `coral-deep` for smaller text on cloud. Each pairing clears WCAG contrast; the original `coral` as small text on white does not (2.7:1).

**The Slate-Not-Grey Rule.** Secondary text is `slate`, never a lighter grey. The earlier `#8C93AE` failed contrast at 3.0:1 and is retired.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Bricolage Grotesque (with Arial)
**Body Font:** Hanken Grotesk (with Arial, system sans)
**Label Font:** Space Mono (with Courier New, system monospace)

**Character:** Bricolage gives headlines a confident, slightly quirky grotesque voice that reads like a person making a point; Hanken is a calm, neutral workhorse for evidence; Space Mono is the margin note, used only to label and classify.

### Hierarchy
Values are set for a 1920×1080 presentation canvas, which is where the system was built.
- **Numeral** (800, 300px, 0.8): the faded section number behind an opener title, in Display Coral at 16% opacity. Openers only.
- **Cover** (800, 118px, 0.95): cover and closing statements only.
- **Opener** (700, 88px, 1.0): the title of a section opener; may carry one accent word in Display Coral.
- **Title** (700, 44px, 1.05): the title of every content slide or page section. Written as a sentence that tells the story on its own.
- **Subhead** (700, 30px, 1.1): column heads, lead-ins, the "from → to" pairs (with the "from" half at weight 500).
- **Card head** (700, 24px, 1.2): step names, card titles, node labels, behaviour pills.
- **Lead** (400, 24px, 1.5): the one or two sentences under a title that state the argument.
- **Body** (400 / 700, 21px, 1.45): evidence, lists, table cells, risk text. Single-line table cells may tighten to 1.3. Keep paragraphs to 45–75 characters per line.
- **Gloss** (400 italic, 21px, 1.35): the short italic definition under a term, e.g. "solves it without waiting".
- **Kicker** (Space Mono 700, 24px, +0.15em, uppercase): the section marker above a title ("1 · Vision"), in Text Coral with a short coral rule before it.
- **Label** (Space Mono 700, 18px, +0.12em, uppercase): column headers, "How we get there", risk labels, footers.
- **Tag** (Space Mono 400, 18px, +0.06em, uppercase): Experience example / Hypothesis tags.

**On the web and in portfolios (derived, provisional).** Keep the same roles and ratios, anchored to an 18px body: lead ≈ 1.15× body, title ≈ 2.1× body, opener and cover set with `clamp()` so they scale with the viewport. Labels and tags stay at 0.75–0.8rem, never smaller. Confirm these values when the first web surface is built.

### Named Rules
**The Fixed Scale Rule.** Use only the sizes above. When copy doesn't fit its frame, cut the copy; never shrink the type to fit. Fit-to-frame values (20.4, 22.7, 41.4, 74.25px) are drift.

**The Projection Floor Rule.** Nothing below 18px on the 1920 canvas, and body text at 21px or more. If a layout only works at 19px, the layout is wrong.

**The Leading-by-Role Rule.** Line height belongs to the role, not the frame: lead 1.5, body 1.45, gloss 1.35, labels 1.2, titles 1.05.

**The Short Caps Rule.** Uppercase mono is for labels of four words or fewer. Anything longer is set in Hanken bold, sentence case.

**The Aligned Numbers Rule.** Any column of numbers (targets, metrics, percentages) uses tabular figures so the digits line up.

## Layout

Built on a 1920×1080 canvas with wide, fixed margins: 130px left and right, 88px above the title block and 160px reserved at the bottom for the footer. Each content slide stacks a kicker, a title that tells the story, a hairline, then one of a small set of compositions: two balanced columns, a narrative column on the left with a diagram on the right, three parallel columns, or a full-width table.

Section openers use a fixed pattern: the faded numeral behind a kicker, an opener title with one accent word, a lead line, one or two paragraphs on the left, and a single diagram on the right. All four openers in a deck must share this layout, so the audience always knows when a new section starts.

Spacing steps are 4, 8, 14, 20, 24, 40 and 64px: tight steps inside a group, 40–64px between groups. The footer carries the author name in Slate on the left and the page number in coral on the right; page numbers always match the slide's position.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is flat. There are no drop shadows anywhere. Depth comes from tone and from halo rings: a filled circle node sits inside a solid ring of a lighter tint (blush around coral, periwinkle around navy), drawn as a zero-blur spread, so the diagram reads as layered without lifting off the page.

### Named Rules
**The Flat Paper Rule.** Surfaces never cast shadows. Separate content with a mist or cloud panel, a hairline, or space.

**The Halo Not Glow Rule.** Rings are solid, zero-blur and a tint of the node's own colour. Blurred or coloured glows are not part of the system.

## Shapes

Two shape families carry the whole system. **Pills** (fully rounded, `pill`) for anything that labels or classifies: tags, behaviour pills, roadmap bars, step markers. **Circles** (`node`) for anything that is a stage or an actor in a diagram: loop steps, the behaviour/products pair, the craft roles around the test bed. Containers are gently rounded rectangles: `card` (14px) for cards and risk boxes, `panel` (20px) for the dark "first move" panel. Borders are 1px hairlines; the only dashed line is the Hypothesis tag.

## Components

### Evidence tags
The system's signature: every claim can say where it comes from.
- **Experience example:** navy pill, white mono text (`tag-experience`). On navy surfaces it inverts to white with navy text (`tag-experience-on-navy`).
- **Hypothesis:** transparent pill with a 2px dashed coral outline; the text takes the surface's text colour.
- **Risk:** blush pill with a navy uppercase label (`tag-risk`).

### Kicker
Short coral rule (3px high), then the section marker in Kicker type and Text Coral: "1 · Vision", "3 · Driving change".

### Behaviour pill
Navy pill with white Card-head type (`behaviour-pill`). Variants use coral, periwinkle (navy text) or slate fills to tell four behaviours apart.

### Diagram nodes
Circles in navy (`node-ink`) or coral (`node-coral`) with Card-head labels, each inside its halo ring. Outcome or goal nodes are coral; actors and steps are navy or outlined.

### Risk box
Blush card (`risk-box`) with a "Risk" label, the risk in bold body text, then "Mitigation:" and the response in regular body text. Every risk box carries a mitigation line.

### First move panel
Navy panel (`panel-ink`) with a coral label, white body text and a closing question in Display Coral.

### Callout
A quote or key takeaway set in Lead type, with a 1px coral rule on the left and 24px inset. Never a thick bar.

### Metrics table
Full-width rows divided by hairlines; area labels in bold, signals in body, values in the Numeric role with tabular figures; the furthest-out column in bold.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** write every title as a sentence that tells the story, so someone flicking through gets the argument from titles alone.
- **Do** tag evidence: Experience example for what you've done, Hypothesis for what you'd test, Risk with a mitigation.
- **Do** choose coral by background, per the Coral-by-Background Rule.
- **Do** keep body text at 21px or more on slides and the type scale fixed.
- **Do** give every section opener the same layout: numeral, kicker, opener title, lead, one diagram.
- **Do** use tabular figures for any column of numbers.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use `coral` (#F07A6A) as small text on a light background.
- **Don't** use drop shadows, blurred glows or gradients for depth.
- **Don't** use a thick coloured bar on the side of a card or callout; 1px is the maximum.
- **Don't** set sentences in uppercase mono.
- **Don't** shrink type to fit a frame; cut the copy instead.
- **Don't** use monospace for anything that isn't a label, kicker, tag or number.
