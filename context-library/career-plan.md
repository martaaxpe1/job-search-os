## Addressing-Weaknesses Analysis

### Weakness 1: Industry generalist — no deep domain specialisation
**The gap:** Marta has worked across water, FMCG, fintech, big tech, aviation, maritime, and AI. For roles at companies hiring for specific domain expertise (food innovation at Nestlé, energy innovation at Shell), the breadth can read as shallow rather than versatile.
**How to address:**
- Lead with *capability* depth (12 years of discovery + experimentation methodology), not domain depth
- For food/water roles: foreground Culligan (water category creation, sustainability mission, consumer behaviour research) and Clorox/Poett (FMCG consumer insight → market launch)
- For sustainability roles: foreground Culligan's Give Power partnership, the plastic waste reduction mission of Pelican, and Delft behavior-change research
- For big tech roles: foreground Google (Help Center, 12-month programme) and YouTube (CS journey design) to establish pattern recognition
- Frame generalism as an asset explicitly: "I bring cross-industry pattern recognition — I've seen how ABN AMRO validated monetization, how Culligan built IoT from scratch, how Google manages localisation at scale. That perspective is rare in someone who also executes."

### Weakness 2: Culligan is not a recognised brand name
**The gap:** Culligan is a large company ($1B+ revenue, global operations) but most European interviewers won't know it immediately. This creates a 10-second credibility gap before they Google it.
**How to address:**
- Always add a brief qualifier on the CV and in interviews: "Culligan — the global leader in drinking water solutions, operating in 90+ countries"
- Lead with the *work*, not the company: "I built a new product category from scratch with $2.5M projected revenue and established the company's IoT infrastructure" lands before the name matters
- Use the Google and YouTube projects (via Unspoken) as the brand-name anchor in the CV — list them prominently
- IKEA, Vueling, ABN AMRO/Tikkie, Philips, Google, YouTube — these are all recognisable; make sure they're visible

### Weakness 3: Overqualification risk for IC roles
**The gap:** Marta's last 3 years were spent leading a team of ~15, owning a budget, doing capital appropriation requests, and reporting to global leadership. When applying for Senior Service Designer or Lead UX Researcher roles, hiring managers may worry she'll be bored, demand more authority than the role offers, or leave quickly.
**How to address:**
- Be honest and proactive in interviews: "I'm deliberately choosing to step into a hands-on IC role because [specific reason relevant to that company] — I want to be closer to the craft and embedded in execution, not managing up"
- Reframe the management experience as additive, not threatening: "Having led a team means I know how to work *with* research and design leadership, not around it. I understand what good looks like from both sides."
- Emphasise the IC work within the leadership roles: the Pelican UX/UI design, the YouTube diary studies and immersions, the ABN AMRO experiment design — these are hands-on deliverables, not just oversight
- Acknowledge the junior colleague concern privately; in interviews, frame it as: "I enjoy mentoring and I learn from people at all levels — some of the sharpest thinking I've seen came from junior researchers"
- For IC applications: consider whether to include budget ownership and team size prominently, or to foreground the hands-on craft work instead
