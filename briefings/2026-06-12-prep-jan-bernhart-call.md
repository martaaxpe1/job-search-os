## Process questions — what to extract from Jan

Ask naturally across the conversation, not as a list:

- "What's the client specifically emphasising in their brief — is it more the design systems side, the hardware-software handoff, or the team leadership?" — surfaces what they're actually hiring for vs. what's in the JD
- "How far into the process are they? Have they seen candidates they liked but didn't progress?" — tells you what's been rejected and why
- "How many candidates are you putting forward, and what does your selection look like?" — tells you how competitive the shortlist is
- "What does the interview process look like from here — stages, who's involved, timeline?" — practical, shows you're serious
- "Is there a portfolio review at any stage, or is it more interview-led?" — lets you prepare the right materials

---

## The objection probe

Don't wait for him to raise concerns — ask directly. Shows self-awareness and gives you a chance to address things before they become blockers.

"Based on what you know of the client's brief and my background — where do you think the friction points are? I'd rather know now so we can address them."

Then listen without defending. Whatever he says, respond with: "That makes sense — here's how I'd frame that..." not "Actually I do have that experience."

If he raises the prior direct rejection:
"Yes, I applied directly in May. I think the positioning was wrong — I led with innovation strategy when the role is fundamentally about physical-digital craft leadership. The work is the same, but the framing matters. That's why I wanted to come through you with a clearer angle."

Jan's job is to place candidates. If he puts you forward and it goes well, he gets paid. You're not asking him for a favour — you're giving him a strong candidate for a hard brief. Go in as a peer, not a supplicant.
