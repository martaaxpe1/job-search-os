# Telling Rabobank: timing, the call, the emails

**The short version:** call Anne first, then confirm by email the same day. Do it the morning after bol confirms in writing. Give her one true reason (the role), apologise once, thank her specifically, and name bol. No numbers, and no comments on scale 09 or her email unless she asks.

---

## 1. Timing

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Today, Mon 26/10** | If you haven't replied to Anne yet, send the holding line below. Email Thijmen your points if that hasn't gone yet |
| **Tue 27 – Wed 28/10** | Thijmen answers. You accept bol in writing and he confirms |
| **The next morning (aim for Thu 29/10)** | Book 10 minutes with Anne, call her, send the confirmation email, email Sylvana, message Youp. All on the same day |
| **Fri 30/10** | The latest Anne hears from you |

**Holding line to Anne, today:**

> Thanks, Anne, that's clear, and thank you for going through each point. I'll come back to you with my decision by Friday 30 October.

Friday rather than Thursday gives you a day of buffer in case bol is slow. Missing a second date would cost you more goodwill than giving her a later one now.

**What "secure at bol" means.** bol's offer is already in writing. Once you've accepted it in writing and Thijmen has confirmed, you're safe to tell Rabobank. You don't need the signed contract in hand.

- **Don't wait for the Validata screening.** It can take a week or two, and Rabobank needs that time more than you do. What makes it safe is that your CV dates match what referees will say, so check that first (bol analysis, section 7).
- **If Thijmen's answer on the bonus point slips past Wednesday,** decide whether it's a condition for you. If it isn't, accept without it and keep Anne's date. If it is, you may have to ask Anne for a few more days, and that costs goodwill. Better to know now than on Thursday.

## 2. Call or email

**Call, then email.** Anne backed you through the process. She rescheduled after your connection dropped, and she made you the offer by phone. She's about to lose a hire three weeks before the start. If the news only comes by email after you've accepted, it looks like you're avoiding her. The email is there for the record and for Sylvana.

**Book the call** by replying on her thread, or by WhatsApp if you have her number:

> Hi Anne, do you have 10 minutes for a call this morning? I'd like to talk you through my decision.

This will tell her what's coming. That's kinder than surprising her.

- No reply in a couple of hours: call her directly.
- She doesn't pick up: don't leave the news on voicemail. Text her to ask for a call back.
- No call by the end of the day: send the fallback email (section 5). Don't let it run into a second day.

Keep it to the morning, and not on a Friday afternoon if you can help it.

---

## 3. The call

Get to the news in the first 30 seconds. Small talk first makes it worse.

**Open**
> Thanks for making time, Anne. I'll get straight to it, because I wanted you to hear it from me. I've decided not to join Rabobank. I've accepted an offer from bol.

**The reason, in one or two sentences**
> It's a principal role, leading the UX craft across their product teams. It's a step up from anything I've done, and I couldn't let it pass.

**The apology, once**
> I'm really sorry. I know I accepted, and I know this comes three weeks before the start, after everything you put into the process.

**The thanks, specific.** Use what's true for you:
> You rescheduled when my connection dropped in September and you backed me all the way through. I really appreciated that.

**Then stop and let her talk.**

**Close**
> I'll send you a short email today to confirm, and I'll let Sylvana know so she can stop the paperwork.

### If she says…

| Anne | You |
|---|---|
| "Is there anything we can do?" / "What would it take?" | *"That's kind of you, thank you. No. I've accepted, and it's about the role."* **Decide before the call that the answer is no.** If you hesitate, she'll hear an opening, and turning down a counter-offer later is worse for both of you |
| "Was it the money?" / "Was it the scale?" | *"Mainly the role. It's a level up. The scale played a part too: the vacancy was scale 10, and without a path or a date to get there, the gap was bigger."* Only say the second part if it's true for you. It's useful feedback for her, as long as you don't put it as a complaint |
| "Did you have this when you accepted?" / "So that's why you hadn't signed." | *"I was in the final stage with them when I accepted, and I didn't know if it would come through. I accepted because I wanted to join. The questions I sent you were real, and your answers did count. I should have told you I was still in another process."* The last sentence is your call. It's honest, and it takes the sting out of the thing she's most likely to feel |
| "Which company?" | bol. She'll see it on LinkedIn anyway |
| "We've already let other candidates go" / "Onboarding has started" | *"I know, and I'm sorry. That's why I wanted to tell you as early as I could."* Then stop |
| "But you accepted." (more likely from Sylvana) | *"I did, and I'm sorry to withdraw it. I haven't signed the contract, and I'm telling you three weeks before the start so you have as much time as possible."* |
| "Good luck" / "The door's open" | *"Thank you. I'd like to stay in touch."* |
| She's short or cold | Let her be. Don't fill the silence with more apologies. *"I understand. Thank you for taking the call."* |

### Don't

- **Give bol's numbers or terms.** If she asks: *"It's a higher level, and the package reflects that."*
- **Bring up her email,** "trust our system", or the change from the soft-landing framing. It did affect the decision, but raised on this call it sounds like blame.
- **Open with "maybe in the future".** It sounds like you're keeping Rabobank as a backup. If she offers to stay in touch, say yes.
- **Apologise more than once, or say it was a hard decision more than once.** Once each is true. More than that and the call becomes about your guilt, not her news.
