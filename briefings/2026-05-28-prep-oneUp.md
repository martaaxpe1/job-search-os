### 3. "How do you see the difference between in-house innovation and consulting?"

They know you've been in-house. They're hiring you to consult. This question tests whether you understand the shift.

**Script:** "In-house, your biggest advantage is time and continuity — you can run a three-year programme, build trust with business units slowly, own the outcome all the way to scale. Your biggest disadvantage is that you're inside the political system — every experiment has internal stakeholders who have a reason to see it fail. Consulting flips both. You have less time and you have to build trust fast. But you also have outside perspective, which is genuinely valuable when internal teams have been looking at the same problem for too long. ### 4. "How would you approach a new client engagement in food?"

This is a mini case question — they're testing whether you think like a consultant, not just a researcher.

**Script:** "My first move is always to understand what the client already knows versus what they think they know. So I'd start by mapping both sides: what does the consumer want, and what does the business actually have the capability to deliver? Then I'd use that gap to define where the interesting innovation territory is. The Culligan experience taught me that the most fundable innovations sit at the intersection of genuine consumer need and a commercial model the business can actually sustain.
