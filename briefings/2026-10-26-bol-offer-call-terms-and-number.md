## 1. The terms recruiters don't usually volunteer

Thijmen will probably lead with base, bonus, leave days and "75% of the pension premium". Everything below is what's usually left out. One line makes all of it routine:

> I'm comparing two full packages line by line, so I have some specific questions. Some may be easier to answer in writing.

### A. Ask on the call (these move the number)

1. **"Is that gross per month, with the 8% holiday allowance on top? Any 13th month?"**
2. **"What's the full band for this level, and where does this sit in it?"**
3. **"Is it 40 hours? Is 36 possible in this role?"**
4. **"What's the total pension premium as a % of salary, and what's the offset?"** Then: *"So my 25% share comes to roughly what % of salary?"* Your share comes off your net pay every month, so it cuts the real value of the base.
5. **"Bonus: what's the target and the maximum, what decides it, and what has it actually paid out the last two or three years?"** Then: *"With a November start, do I count for 2026 at all?"*
6. **"When is the next salary review, and am I in it if I start in November?"** A Q4 start often skips the first review cycle, which means a full year at the starting base.
7. **"Is there a lease car or mobility budget at this level?"** bol mentioned an EV lease budget. Also ask the monthly home-working allowance (Rabobank pays about €83).
8. **"Is there a long-term incentive or share plan at this level, through Ahold Delhaize?"** Probably not, but it costs nothing to ask.
9. **"Permanent from day one? How long is probation?"**
10. **"Does bol follow a specific CAO? If so, does it apply at this level, or is this role above the CAO scales?"** With a CAO, the salary scales, the yearly raises and the leave terms are in a public document you can read today, and the raises are agreed collectively, the way Rabobank's are. Without one, everything is in bol's own terms of employment, so the documents in B matter even more. Senior roles are often above the CAO scales even when a CAO exists, so ask both halves.

**Growth and reviews.** This is the comparison point with Anne's reply this morning, which gave no criteria and no date for scale 10. Listen for whether bol's answer is any more concrete.

11. **"How are growth paths normally defined for a role like this? It's new and principal-level, so what does the next step look like, and who decides?"** A good answer names a level above principal and what it takes to get there. "We'll shape it together" means there's no path yet. That's fair for a new role, but then the six-month review in section 2 matters more.
12. **"How do performance reviews work for this kind of role: how often, who runs them, and what are they based on?"** Then: *"With no team of my own, whose input counts: Ronald, the Heads of Craft, the designers?"* and *"How does the review link to salary increases and the bonus?"* In an influence role, your reviewer and the people they ask decide your raise, so this is also how you find out the reporting line.

**Family questions, if you want them live.** In the comparison frame they read as benefits questions:

13. **"Does bol top up the UWV maternity benefit to 100% of actual salary, for salaries above the UWV cap?"** UWV pays only up to its daily-wage cap (roughly €80K a year). At bol's salary that's about two-thirds of your pay without a top-up. Rabobank pays 100% for all 16 weeks. *(Correction to the 20/10 checklist: its "about 80%" was the figure at Rabobank's salary. At bol's salary it's lower.)*
14. **"Does bol top up the 9 paid weeks of parental leave?"** UWV pays 70% of the daily wage, with the same cap, so under half your pay at bol's level. Rabobank tops up weeks 1–4 to 100%.
15. **"During unpaid parental leave, does pension keep building up?"** This is often the hidden cost. Many schemes stop building pension during unpaid leave unless the employer keeps paying the premium.

If you'd rather not ask these live, skip them: the documents in B cover all three.

### B. Ask him to send (these are documents, not questions)

> Could you send the offer together with the draft contract, the terms of employment, the bonus rules, and the Pensioen 1-2-3 for the pension scheme?

The **Pensioen 1-2-3** is the standard summary every Dutch pension scheme has. Recruiters often can't answer pension questions in detail, so asking for this saves you from a vague answer.

Also ask: *"If there's a career framework or level description for the craft roles, could you send that too?"* If one exists, it answers question 11 in writing. If bol has a CAO (question 10), ask for that too.

### C. Check in the documents (by Wed 28/10)

**Pension and insurance**
- Defined contribution? Has the scheme moved to the new pension system yet? (All schemes have to switch by 1/1/2028.)
- Are the holiday allowance and bonus pensionable?
- **WIA excess (disability) insurance.** State disability cover stops at the same ~€80K cap. At bol's salary, about a third of your income is uninsured without it. Big employers usually have it; check.
- Partner's pension, if relevant to you.

**Sickness**
- Sick pay: 100% in year one? Year two? The legal minimum is 70% for two years.

**Family**
- Maternity top-up, parental leave top-up, pension during unpaid leave (questions 13–15).
- Up to what age of the child parental leave can be taken. The legal minimum is 8. Rabobank allows up to 12.
- How leave periods count toward the bonus and the salary review.
- Short-term care leave: 70% (legal) or topped up?

**Contract clauses**
- **Non-compete or non-solicitation clause.** Matters if you go back to freelancing or consulting later.
- **Side activities.** Is freelancing outside bol's sector allowed, and what's the approval process?
- **IP clause.** Does it claim work you do outside bol hours?
- Notice period, both ways.
- Probation length (the legal maximum on a permanent contract is 2 months).

**Flexibility**
- The 8 weeks working from abroad: does Spain count, can it be one block, any tax or social-security conditions?
- 29 days of leave: is that at 40h? Can you buy extra days?

## 2. Reacting to the number

### Step 1: Take it in, say it back

Write it down. Then, neutral and warm:

> Thank you, that's good to hear. Just so I have it right: €X gross per month, 40 hours, with holiday allowance on top?

No "wow", no visible disappointment. A two-second pause is fine. Dutch recruiters expect you to think.

If he gives a yearly figure, ask whether it includes holiday allowance. Divide by 12.96 to get the monthly base.

### Step 2: Don't answer the base yet

> Before I react to the number, can we go through the rest of the package?

Then ask questions 4–12. Whether to push depends on pension and hours, and this buys you time to look at the table in section 3. The review answer (question 12) also tells you whether the six-month-review fallback in step 4 is realistic.

### Step 3: Make one ask

| Base | What you say |
|---|---|
| **€9,500 or more** | No ask on base. *"Thank you, that's a strong offer."* Use your one ask on terms (step 4, option 1 or 3). |
| **€9,000–9,499** | *"In our first call I mentioned 9,000 to 9,500. Given the scope Ronald described, I'd like to land at 9,500."* |
| **Below €9,000** | The same line, then: *"Where does this sit in the band?"* |

If the pension turns out low (under ~10% employer at 40h), give the honest reason:

> With a 40-hour week and the pension at that level, 9,500 is what makes the package work for me.

Then stop talking. No "if that's okay", no "sorry to push". Ronald's debrief flagged apologetic closers. This is where they cost you money.

### Step 4: His answer

**"I'll check with Ronald / with comp."**
> Thanks. Could you include the outcome in the written offer?

**He meets you halfway (say €9,250).**
> Thanks, that helps. Could you send it in writing with the full terms? I'll come back to you by Monday.

Don't counter again on the call. You keep one more round for after the documents (step 5).

**"There's no room on base" / "that's the top of the band."** Pick one, in this order:
1. **A review at six months, in writing.** *"If the base is fixed, could we put a review at six months in writing, with a step to 9,500 if I'm hitting the year-one goals Ronald and I discussed?"* Best option: it ties the raise to criteria he already set, and it's cheap for them to agree to.
2. **Year-one bonus.** *"With a November start I miss this year's bonus. Could 2027 be guaranteed at target?"*
3. **Training budget.** *"Ronald mentioned training for influencing at scale. Could we fix a budget for that in the offer?"*

**"If we get to 9,500, are you in?"**
> If we land there and the written terms check out, I can give you an answer quickly.

**"What's the other offer?"**
> It's a solid offer, especially on pension and leave. What matters most to me is the role, and that's where bol is ahead.

Never give Rabobank's base. €6,800 is far below bol's range, and that one number would end your ask.

**"Which way are you leaning?"**
> The role is the strongest part of bol's offer for me. I want to see the written terms before I decide.

**"We need an answer by [date]."**
> That works if I have the full terms by Wednesday.

### Step 5: Keep one round in reserve

At the close:

> If anything in the written terms changes the picture, I'll come back to you on it.

That lets you raise the base again by email if the pension, sick pay or family terms turn out weak. Pension and leave are company policy, so you won't change them. Use them as the reason for a higher base instead.

### Don't
- Accept or decline on the call.
- Negotiate the pension % or leave top-ups directly.
- Mention Anne's reply or the scale 10 situation.
- Explain the ask with personal reasons. The reason is the scope and what you said in September.
