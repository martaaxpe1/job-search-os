### 4.1 The spine
> **From screens to intent.** Designers stop producing screens and start writing the intent (language, principles, evidence) that people and agents build from. The time this frees goes to discovery, where prototypes on real code with real data are how teams decide what to build.

**Three shifts**
1. **Screens → intent.** Writing bol's design language down in a form agents can carry is the craft's new core work. *(comment; VBL)*
2. **Handoffs → dialogue.** Artefacts exist to move the conversation forward. Something tangible with real data, made in a day, changes the detail of the conversation. *(friend; WRP sprints; lign.ai flows-not-radius)*
3. **Domains → journeys.** Experience ownership across domains. **Hypothesis, to be tested**, not a prediction. *(friend; braindump)*

**The honest human cost:** cognitive load goes up and people protect their expertise (your friend's #1 risk). That is why designers are the users of this change.

**Why it matters beyond efficiency: democratisation → discovery → innovation**
- Every craft's tools are now open to everyone: data, code, design, research. Anyone with agency can show an idea as a working experience on real data instead of describing it. Rarely a static screen any more, and always framed as a hypothesis to prove.
- Cycles are shortening everywhere, not only in delivery. The real choice is what to do with the time: ship more of the same faster, or discover, think and challenge more.
- **The innovation argument (for Maurice):** a more discovery-oriented product organisation explores more of bol's own future. Proof line, from your own work: *at Culligan I paused the smart bottle because we couldn't hold two horizons at once (Story 11). Shorter cycles mean fewer good ideas die for lack of capacity. We should still kill darlings, but because the evidence says so, not because we ran out of time.*
- Guardrail: tie the innovation argument to hypothesis-driven discovery with evidence. Otherwise it can sound like "designers want time to play," especially against a 6-hours-back KR.

### 4.7 Layer 4 craft inventory: what the toolkits actually specify

Source: VBL v2.0, layers 4 and 3. Attribution per your 02/10 split. **Yours:** personas, journeys, context-based UX character. **Co-designed:** touchscreen and indicator toolkits, accessibility. **Ammunition:** visual UI, motion and micro-interaction execution. **Yours:** review and approval. Phrase motion as: *"I defined what motion and light had to mean; the agency designed how it looked."*

**Interaction logic (the rules)**
- **Steps to dispense:** with 1-2 options, selecting *is* dispensing; with 3+, selection and dispense are separate steps, in that order.
- **Option hierarchy:** one option gets centred; two are shown equally; three or more get a hierarchy; six or more get nested in categories.
- **Defaults by context:** in shared spaces, revert to the most-used option after each session; at home, remember the last choice.
- **Speed to water:** in rushed contexts, use preset amounts so people can multitask while the vessel fills.
- **Central interaction area:** dispense point, main controls, displays and high-priority errors sit together. The drip tray, filters, settings and low-priority messages go to a secondary area.
- **Dials:** with 4 or fewer options the scroll stops at the ends; with 5 or more it loops. Notch spacing is inversely proportional to dial size.
- **Ergonomics:** one-handed dispense; works for either dominant hand (the vessel is often in the dominant hand).
- **Navigation:** always know where to start and what's next; know where you are and how to return; progressive disclosure (swipe up, pagination, QR/NFC for depth).
- **Idle state:** keep the offer on screen while asleep, or wake on proximity, so the device feels "alive, welcoming the user."

**Colour and light, synced across hardware and screen**
- **Azure has one meaning on both materials.** In CMF (layer 3) it's the accent for critical touch points and interfaces. On screen (layer 4) it signals Culligan's Firewall technology and the transformation happening inside.
- **UI palette extended:** red for hot water; the tertiary palette used sparingly for calls to action and alerts.
- **Indicator states**, the same on LEDs and screen: white means on, all well; yellow means attention, not urgent; orange means act now.
- **Physical buttons speak the screen's selection language:** back-lit icons when selected; half brightness for "available but not selected"; embossed or debossed icons where back-lighting isn't possible.
- **Light as guidance:** lighting to locate the nozzle; spotlight and runway-light pour targets on the drip tray.
- **One icon system** across screens and physical buttons (shared grid, rounded stroke ends, emphasis on curves). Manrope in four weights, plus a low-res character set for very small displays.
- **One UX across hardware tiers:** BFC Good/Better/Best faceplates go from matte plastic to back-painted glass, with screens designed across all tiers.
- **UX requirements drove hardware choice:** screens were sourced for brightness, viewing angle, contrast, resolution, colour range and light sensitivity.

**Motion and feedback (meaning defined; execution by Ammunition)**
- **Movement reflects urgency:** static information stays calm; pulsing or blinking only when attention is critical.
- **"Always working" feeling:** breathing or pulsing light, and cycles or easing, so people know the product is working in the background.
- **On-screen feedback:** hold states, micro-animations and immediate action. Physical buttons confirm through travel, sound or haptics.
- **Animations only when useful,** extending logically from the interaction, and they can be reduced or switched off (motion sensitivity).

**Language and copy**
- **Background products:** "Straightforward" and "Proactive" mean clear language about what's going on and whether anything needs doing, and never leaving the user to wonder what's next.
- **Escalating information:** concise error codes → displays with next-step instructions → interactive messages on the touchscreen.
- **Sound-only dispense flow** (scripted conversation): the device wakes and announces Voice Control, reads the formats, asks which format and how much, confirms aloud, asks the user to hold the vessel and say "Dispense", then says "All done."
- **Sustainability on screen:** a "Bottles Saved" counter and environmental information in the idle state.
- **Gap:** detailed screen microcopy is *not* in this document. If you wrote it, speak to it from memory; don't present it as specified here.

**Accessibility (co-designed)**
- **90/10:** a single inclusive baseline, plus targeted features.
- **Designed beyond formal disabilities:** gloves and PPE, migraines, arthritis, ageing eyesight, lighting conditions.
- **BFC specifics:** settings a servicer or facilities manager can configure (contrast, text size, arrow buttons, read format); user-facing toggles; next/previous switching instantly; reduced animations; extra-large text; testing across ages, abilities and lighting.

**Three craft moments for the case (~45 sec total, with screens from the doc)**
1. **"One colour, one meaning, on plastic and pixels."** Azure + the white/yellow/orange states + half-brightness back-lighting.
2. **"Motion that tells the truth about urgency."** Calm vs pulsing, plus the "always working" breathing light. It's also the bridge to bol: agents must signal urgency honestly too.
3. **"Rules that come from real use."** Steps-to-dispense, and shared-vs-home defaults (from your context-based character). These are the *rules* in the rules/choices/character table.

Backup, if they ask about conversation design: the sound-only dispense flow.
