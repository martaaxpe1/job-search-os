## 2. Questions, in order of what decides it

Skip anything he already covered on 14/09 or covers on the call.

**Must have**
1. **Base, and where it sits in the band for this level.** What's the top of the band?
2. **Hours.** 40 or 36?
3. **Employer pension contribution, as a % of the pension base.** "75% of the premium" isn't enough to compare; ask what the premium is, and the offset.
4. **Bonus.** Target and max, what it's based on, whether year one is prorated, and what it actually paid out the last two or three years.
5. **Holiday allowance on top of base? 13th month?**
6. **Contract.** Permanent from day one, or a first fixed-term year? Probation length?
7. **Side activities.** Can you keep freelancing outside bol's sector, and does it need approval?
8. **Full terms in writing.** The employee handbook or terms-of-employment document, not only the offer letter. Covers maternity and parental leave top-ups without asking about them directly:

> Could you send the full terms of employment along with the offer? I'd like to compare the whole package, pension and leave included.

9. **Start date**, and whether it's flexible.

**Good to have**
10. Who you'd report to, if that isn't settled yet.
11. Training. Ronald mentioned training for influencing at scale; ask what that looks like and whether there's a personal budget.
12. Home-working allowance, mobility, notice period.

## 3. The number

In round 1 you told Thijmen "the top of the band, around 9,000, 9,500." Hold that.

| Offer base | What you do |
|---|---|
| **€9,500 or more** | Don't push on base. Spend your asks on terms (contract type, start date, training). |
| **€9,000 to €9,499** | Ask once: *"In our first call I mentioned 9,000 to 9,500. Given the scope Ronald described, I'd like to land at 9,500."* |
| **Below €9,000** | Same line. It's below what you told him in September, so it's a fair correction, not a new demand. Then ask where the offer sits in the band. |

Say the line, then stop. No "I hope that's okay" after it.

Why €9,500: at that base bol beats Rabobank on total yearly pay even with zero pension, and per hour worked at 40h it needs only 9% employer pension. At €9,000 it needs 16% to match per hour.

**If he asks about the other offer:** name its strengths, not its figures.

> It's a solid offer, especially on pension and leave. What matters most to me is the role, and that's where bol is ahead.

Rabobank's base is far below bol's posted range, so never compare base salaries. Pension (21.5% employer) and full-pay leave are where Rabobank is strong, and they're exactly what you need bol to spell out.

**If he asks "if we get to 9,500, are you in?":**

> If we land there and the written terms check out, I can give you an answer quickly.
