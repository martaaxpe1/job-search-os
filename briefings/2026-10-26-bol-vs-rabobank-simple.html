Assumptions

  • Rabobank CAO raise: 3% a year from 2027. It isn't 6% every year: the 6% (4% in January 2026, 2% in June 2026) was for the CAO that runs to 31/12/2026. The next CAO is still being negotiated. The unions are asking for 5% for 2027; Rabobank says its costs are too high.
  • Rabobank individual raise: 2.5% each April (a "successful year"; 5% for an exceptional year, 0% if improvement is needed), until the scale maximum. Your €6,800 is €383 below the scale 09 maximum (€7,183), so you'd hit the ceiling in 2029.
  • Rabobank scale 10: +5% on promotion, scale maximum €8,563. Anne gave no date, so this is a scenario, not a promise.
  • bol raises: none in 2027, then 4% a year, merit-based, decided by management in calibration. bol has no CAO. Thijmen talked about 20–30% growth in the band "over the years".
  • bol bonus: 95% of the 25% target, based on Thijmen's "3–5% below target on average".
  • Left out: bol's lease car or allowance (up to €14K a year gross, not confirmed), a possible bol move to scale 20, and Rabobank's €1,400 development budget.
  • Pension uses each scheme's 2026 rules. Rabobank: 21.5% employer. bol: BPF Detailhandel up to €79,409, BeFrank above.
  • Pension, year 1: at the starting salary, before any raise. Rabobank's pensionable income is 12 months' salary plus holiday pay and 13th month (€94,928), minus a €18,475 franchise. bol's assumes holiday pay is pensionable (€120,528); if it isn't, about €1.2K a year less goes in.
  • Net pay: 2026 Dutch tax rules (35.75%, 37.56% and 49.5%, with the general and labour tax credits), after your own pension contribution, and no other deductions such as a mortgage or partner. Rabobank's benefit budget is counted as cash; spent on tax-free options it nets more. At these salaries each extra gross euro keeps only about 44 cents, because the labour tax credit tapers off on top of the 49.5% rate. bol pays holiday pay in May, so its regular months are a little below the average shown; Rabobank spreads holiday pay and the 13th month over the year.
