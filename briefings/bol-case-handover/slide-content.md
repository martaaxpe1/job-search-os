## Story in one breath

AI is changing what a designer's day is made of. The vision is a bol designer who works as a maker and spends most of their time on discovery, together with every craft. That only works if people change how they behave, so the standard is a behaviour framework (high agency, taste, curiosity, adaptability), made practical through one loop and one standard ("show it working"). It lands in six months through one squad, sceptics first, a shared test bed with the other crafts, and by giving the Heads of Design their time back first. Success means behaviour changes first, and products follow.

## Slide 07 · Each behaviour is observable, and bol owes something back

**On the slide**

- 2 · Standards & frameworks
- Behaviour
- What it looks like
- What it isn't
- How we make it possible
- High agency solves it without waiting
- Builds a working prototype on real data to test a hypothesis, instead of asking for resources first. Brings a proposal with the problem.
- Working alone, or bypassing other crafts
- Access to tools, repositories and data. Permission to run small experiments within clear guardrails.
- High taste knows what good looks like, and why
- Rejects plausible but generic output and can explain why. Writes intent others, and agents, can use. Knows what to remove.
- Personal preference, or pixel policing
- A shared design language that captures intent. Critiques built on real examples.
- High curiosity reaches for the tool before the expert
- Tries the new tool or workflow first, and shares where it failed. Goes looking for data and user evidence.
- Chasing every new tool
- Protected time to explore. An open craft lab to share findings.
- High adaptability changes course when the evidence says so
- Changes their mind in public. Works across crafts when the work needs it. Comfortable moving between many topics.
- No direction, or constant pivoting
- Short cycles with exit criteria agreed upfront. Psychological safety to be wrong.
- [Experience example] Developing Alex at Culligan: creating explicit moments in design reviews where his field knowledge was the deciding voice, until challenging senior stakeholders became normal for him. He now runs a full category.

**Speaker notes**

> Each behaviour has a "looks like", an "isn't", and what the organisation has to provide. The last column is my job: if we ask for agency, we owe people access and permission.

## Slide 08 · One loop turns the behaviours into daily work

**On the slide**

- 2 · Standards & frameworks
- Example: one designer's itch, through the loop [Hypothesis]
- "I spend two days making variations for a review, and the decision still comes down to opinion."
- Frame
- What does the review need to decide, and what would prove it?
- Explore
- AI lists edge cases and gaps in the options, instead of producing more variations.
- Make
- One working prototype on real data, in a day.
- Decide
- The review discusses the prototype and the evidence. The decision and its reason are written down.
- Encode
- What we learned becomes intent in the design language, so the next draft starts closer.
- Observe
- After release, live behaviour shows whether it holds.
- The standard behind it, "show it working": every proposal arrives as a working artefact, with its hypothesis and the real data it was tested on.
- [Experience example] I replaced research reports with design sprints; developers started asking for the format at kick-off.

**Diagram labels** (inline SVG)

**Risk box**
- Risk: Simulated users and AI-found edge cases get mistaken for real research.
- Mitigation: Simulated evidence is labelled separately, and decisions that matter still go to real users.

**Speaker notes**

> The behaviours become practical through one loop. The example is a common itch, days of variations that end in opinion. Through the loop, it becomes one working prototype, an evidence-based decision, and intent the next draft can use. The same theme comes back in my first move, one level up, for the Heads of Design.
